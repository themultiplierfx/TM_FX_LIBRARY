ATOMIC STRUCTURE 1

JOHN DALTON’S THEORY

  1. All elements are made up of small, indivisible particle called ATOMS.

  2. Atoms can neither be created nor destroyed.

  3. Atoms of the same element are alike in all aspects and differ from atoms of every other element.

  4. When atoms combine, they do so in a small whole ratio.

  5. Chemical reactions result from the combination or separation of atoms.

ADVANTAGES OF DALTON’S THEORY

  1. Elements were represented with symbols. e.g. Mg, H, O, Na

  2. Compounds were represented with formulae. e.g. H2O

  3. Chemical reactions were represented with equations. e.g. 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O

DISADVANTAGE

  1. John Dalton’s theory could not explain the formation of ion or electrolysis.

MODIFICATION TO JOHN DALTON’S THEORY

  1. The discovery of the sub-atomic particles (protons, Neutron & electrons ) shows that an atom is divisible.

  2. The study of radioactivity or nuclear chemistry reveals that an atom can be destroyed.

  3. The phenomenon of Isotopy shows that the atoms of the same element can be different.

  4. Lastly, except for ordinary reaction reactions, atoms can be combine in large numbers in organic chemistry.

LAWS THAT SUPPORTED JOHN DALTON’S THEORY.

  • The law of the conservation of matter

  • The law of definite proportion

  • The law of multiple proportion

  • The law of the reciprocal proportion

  1. law of the conservation of matter:

This states that matter can neither be created nor destroyed but can be converted from one form to another.

  1. law of definite proportion:

This states that all pure sample of a particular compound contains the same element combined in the same proportions by mass.

  1. law of multiple proportion:

This states that if two element A and B combine chemically together to form more than one various masses of element A which combine separately with a fixed mass of element B are in simple multiple ratio .

ATOMS AND IONS

  • Atom is formed when proton number is equal to the electron number.

  • When the proton number is not equal to an electron number an ion is formed.

  • The charge of an atom is zero or neutral.

  • When an atom carries a positive or negative charge, it is no longer an atom.

  • An ion, therefore, is defined as an atom or group of atoms which possesses charge.

ISOTOPY

If two atoms have the same atomic number, then, they are atoms of the same element.

  • If two atoms have the same atomic number but different mass number, they are isotopes of the same element.

\[_{17}^{35}{Cl\ \ and\ }_{17}^{37}{Cl\ \ and\ }isopes\]

Isotopy is a phenomenon. In other words, it is a natural occurrence.

\[\mathbf{Why\ do\ we\ have\ isotopes?}\]

DEFINITION

Isotopy is a phenomenon whereby atoms of the same element have the same atomic number but different mass number due to the difference in their neutron number.

Again, it is the difference in the neutron number that is responsible for isotopy.

  • Isotopes of the same element have the same chemical properties.

Isotopes have different physical properties.

IONIC OR ELECTROVALENT BOND

Electrovalent bond is a bond that exists between a metal and a non-metal in which the metal donates its outer most electron(s) to the non- metal so that both of them can attain a duplet or an octet structure or configuration like the noble gases.

There is a complete transfer of electron(s) from the metal to the non-metal.

The more the electronegative difference between a metal and a non-metal, the stronger will be the ionic bond formed between them.

The electronegative difference can be related to the distance between the two elements in the periodic table.

For instance, the distance between group 1 and 7 elements is wider than any other groups. This means, the electronegative difference between group 1 and group 7 is the highest. Therefore, ionic bond between group 1 and group 7 will be the strongest.

KINETIC THEORY OF GASES

  1. Gas molecules consist of small molecules which move randomly in straight lines.

  2. The molecules are perfectly elastic and possess kinetic energy. Hence they are moving randomly colliding with one another and the walls of the containing vessels as such exerting pressure. Energy is not lost during collision but can only be transformed to another form.

  3. The intermolecular forces are negligible and molecules appear to be independent of one another.

  4. The size of the molecules are so small and far apart that the gas occupied a negligible volume compared with that of the container.

  5. The temperature of the gas is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the gas particles.

NOTE

Diffusion is the movement of particles from region of higher concentration through a porous medium to a region of lower concentration. It is fastest in gases than in solid and liquid.

When the moving particle is water, then it is called osmosis. In osmosis, the water moves from higher concentration to a region of lower concentration through semi-permeable membrane.

Effusion is the movement of particles of matter or gases through a small aperture under pressure.

Brownian movement which involves zig -zag or irregular movements of particles, diffusion and osmosis support kinetic theory.

FOUR FACTORS AFFECT THE PERIODICITY OF ELEMENT

  1. Number of valence electron (or outermost electrons)

  2. Nuclear charge

  3. Screening effect of inner electrons

  4. Numbers of shells

These four factors are basically two factors because nuclear charge and screening effect of inner electrons are consequences of the other two factors.

For example

  1. Number of outermost electrons increases nuclear charge

  2. Number of shells increases screening effect of inner electrons

  • The number of shells and the outermost electrons (or valence electrons) cannot increase at the same time. If one increases, the other will be constant and vice versa.

NOTE

  • Nuclear charge is the charge of the nucleus. It is positively charged because the nucleus contains protons.

  • Nuclear charge always brings about attraction. ‘It tends to bring together’

  • Screening effect of inner electrons, on the other hand, is negatively charged.

  • It always brings about repulsion. In other words, ‘It tends to ‘scatter’

Across the period, the number of outermost electrons increases while the number of shells remains the same.

As a result, the nuclear charge which tends to bring together (or the force of attraction) prevails over screening effect of inner electrons.

Consequently, atomic size will reduce and ionization energy will increase across the period.

Even metallic bond increases as the outermost electrons increases across the period.

Increase in nuclear charge favours non-metals. Thus, non -metallic properties increase across the period.

On the other hand, down the group, the number of shells increases while the outermost electrons remain the same.

As a result , the screening effect which tends to scatter (or the force of repulsion) prevails over the nuclear charge.

Consequently, atomic size increases while ionization energy decreases down the group.

Increase in screening effect of inner electrons favours metals. Thus, metallic properties increase down the group.

Note: Metallic bond decreases down the group.

PROPERTIES OF CATHODE RAYS

  1. They travelled in straight lines from the cathode to the anode.

  2. They cast shadows of opaque objects placed in their path.

  3. They are negatively charged particles.

  4. They are capable of producing mechanical motion.

  5. They are identical in nature and in their ratio of charge to mass irrespective of the nature of the residual gas in the discharge tube or metal, used as the cathode.
